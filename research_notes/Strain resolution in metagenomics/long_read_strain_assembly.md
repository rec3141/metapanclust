# Long-read strain-aware metagenome assembly and strain phasing (2018-2026)

Scope note: the search budget was limited and several primary full texts were blocked (PMC returned a captcha; bioRxiv and nature.com returned 429 errors or redirects). Facts below come from the pages that did load: the Strainberry Nature Communications PDF (UEA eprints, full text read), the hifiasm-meta arXiv preprint (full text read), Europe PMC records, GitHub READMEs, the myloasm docs, and search-engine summaries of abstracts. Each finding names its source. Items not checked this session are listed under Gaps.

## Q1. Tool inventory: read type, input, depth, divergence range, number of strains, output, compute

### Takeaway
Two kinds of tool fit a "bin first, then resolve strains" design. **Post-assembly phasers** (Strainberry, Strainy, Floria) take an existing assembly or contigs plus reads, so they can run on a metaFlye/metaMDBG bin. **Strain-aware assemblers** (hifiasm-meta for HiFi; myloasm for HiFi and R10 nanopore at about 97% read accuracy or better) separate strains during assembly. For an existing metaFlye pipeline, Strainy is the natural add-on: it uses a metaFlye `--keep-haplotypes` GFA. Myloasm is the strongest recent claim (strains up to about 99% ANI on R10/HiFi), but its numbers come only from the authors.

### Cited Findings

**Strainberry**
- Citation: Vicedomini R, Quince C, Darling AE, Chikhi R. *Nature Communications* 12:4485 (2021). DOI 10.1038/s41467-021-24515-9. Verified from the PDF header. — [UEA eprint PDF](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/88125/1/Vicedomini_etal_2021_NatureComms.pdf)
- Input: a strain-oblivious assembly (metaFlye or Canu) plus long reads. Each round runs a diploid separation with Longshot (variant calling) and HapCUT2 (phasing). Separated read groups are reassembled with wtdbg2, then scaffolded in a strain-aware way. — [UEA PDF](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/88125/1/Vicedomini_etal_2021_NatureComms.pdf)
- Read types: tested on PacBio CLR (Sequel) and ONT data from 2021 or earlier, in the R9 era. The authors say PacBio "yields better strain-aware assemblies in terms of sequence identity, while Nanopore sequencing data would favor assembly contiguity." — [UEA PDF](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/88125/1/Vicedomini_etal_2021_NatureComms.pdf)
- Depth: >95% reference representation and >99.8% identity "requires at least 20× coverage". Results were "mediocre at 10×". Uneven coverage across conspecific strains did not appear to hurt. The discussion adds: "any strain present at coverage above 10× ends up being correctly separated." — [UEA PDF](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/88125/1/Vicedomini_etal_2021_NatureComms.pdf)
- Divergence: on simulated two-strain E. coli sets (PacBio-like reads), it "fails to separate strains with low strain divergence". Reference coverage improves significantly at 0.39% divergence (about 99.6% ANI), and assembly is near-complete at 0.50% (about 99.5% ANI). — [UEA PDF](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/88125/1/Vicedomini_etal_2021_NatureComms.pdf)
- Number of strains: up to 5 conspecific E. coli strains, with pairwise divergence 0.7-1.4%. Average reference coverage falls from 95% (3 strains) to 75% (5 strains), and ANI from 99.9% to 99.6%. — [UEA PDF](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/88125/1/Vicedomini_etal_2021_NatureComms.pdf)
- Mock3 (PacBio): E. coli K-12 and W share 98.65% identity. metaFlye collapsed them (99.64%/99.74% identity to references, duplication ratio 1.23/1.39). After Strainberry, identity was 99.97% for both with near-complete coverage. In Mock9, two S. aureus strains (97.61% identity) were separated to 99.97% ANI. — [UEA PDF](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/88125/1/Vicedomini_etal_2021_NatureComms.pdf)
- Real data: 20-118% more genomic material for individual strain genomes than conventional assemblies. On the ONT whey-starter (NWC2) data, Lactobacillus strains gained 25-43% more material. Only some strains reached >90% completeness on ONT, which the authors blame on indel errors from that chemistry hurting marker-gene annotation. — [UEA PDF](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/88125/1/Vicedomini_etal_2021_NatureComms.pdf)
- Compute: under 10 GB RAM in all tests. On the human stool (HSM) dataset, Flye took 6.5 h and 132 GB while Strainberry took 4 h and 9.5 GB. — [UEA PDF](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/88125/1/Vicedomini_etal_2021_NatureComms.pdf)
- Stated limits: designed for "low-complexity" metagenomes. Diploid phasing tools assume coverage is split evenly between haplotypes, which does not hold in metagenomes. Contigs may need a variable number of strains along their length. Low-coverage strains are separated into shorter contigs. — [UEA PDF](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/88125/1/Vicedomini_etal_2021_NatureComms.pdf)

**Strainy**
- Citation: Kazantseva E, Donmez A, Frolova M, Pop M, Kolmogorov M. *Nature Methods* 21(11):2034-2043 (2024). DOI 10.1038/s41592-024-02424-1. Preprint: bioRxiv 2023.01.31.526521. — [Europe PMC record](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:39327484%20OR%20DOI:10.1093/bioinformatics/btae252%20OR%20DOI:10.1038/s41592-022-01478-3&resultType=core&format=json); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39327484/)
- Abstract claims: on simulated and mock Nanopore and PacBio data it outperforms current Nanopore-based methods and is comparable to PacBio-based algorithms in completeness and accuracy. Applied to a complex environmental metagenome, it showed distinct strain distribution and mutational patterns. The abstract gives no numbers. — [Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:39327484%20OR%20DOI:10.1093/bioinformatics/btae252%20OR%20DOI:10.1038/s41592-022-01478-3&resultType=core&format=json)
- Input: an assembly graph GFA, which the README says "may be produced with metaFlye or minigraph", or a linear FASTA reference. Also needed: reads, plus an optional BAM and VCF. Recommended assembler call: `flye --meta --keep-haplotypes --no-alt-contigs -i 0`. The `-i 0` disables polishing, which the authors found improves read assignment during minimap2 realignment. — [Strainy GitHub](https://github.com/katerinakazantseva/stRainy)
- Read types: `-m hifi|nano`. The README lists "PacBio HiFi, Nanopore R9 (Guppy5+) and R10." — [Strainy GitHub](https://github.com/katerinakazantseva/stRainy)
- Depth defaults: `--min-unitig-coverage 20` and `--max-unitig-coverage 500`; unitigs outside that range are not phased. Unitigs are split at 50 kb by default because long unitigs "may significantly slow down Strainy". `-d/--cluster-divergence` sets the maximum mismatches per kb in a cluster, and `-a` sets the allele-frequency threshold. — [Strainy GitHub](https://github.com/katerinakazantseva/stRainy)
- Outputs: `strain_contigs.gfa`, `strain_unitigs.gfa`, `alignment_phased.bam` (YC tag), `strain_variants.vcf`, and per-unitig tables. — [Strainy GitHub](https://github.com/katerinakazantseva/stRainy)
- Cost and example: the tutorial (simulated 5-strain E. coli) runs about 2 h on 30 threads and turns about 6.4 Mb of input into 20.6 Mb of strain-specific sequence. The licence is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (non-commercial). README version 1.2. — [Strainy GitHub](https://github.com/katerinakazantseva/stRainy); [search summary of tutorial](https://github.com/katerinakazantseva/strainy)

**Floria**
- Citation: Shaw J, Gounot JS, Chen H, Nagarajan N, Yu YW. *Bioinformatics* 40(Suppl 1):i30-i38 (2024; ISMB 2024 proceedings). DOI 10.1093/bioinformatics/btae252. Preprint: bioRxiv 2024.01.28.577669. — [Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:39327484%20OR%20DOI:10.1093/bioinformatics/btae252%20OR%20DOI:10.1038/s41592-022-01478-3&resultType=core&format=json)
- Method: minimum-error-correction (MEC) read clustering plus a strain-preserving network-flow model. Accepts short or long reads aligned to contigs/references with a VCF. Outputs alleles and read sets per strain ("haplosets"). Floria-PL is an end-to-end pipeline from reads to strain-level assembly. — [Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:39327484%20OR%20DOI:10.1093/bioinformatics/btae252%20OR%20DOI:10.1038/s41592-022-01478-3&resultType=core&format=json); [ISMB slides](https://jim-shaw-bluenote.github.io//jims_assets/ISMB2024-Floria-JimShaw.pdf)
- Performance: more than 3× faster than Strainberry with 21% more strain content recovered (synthetic data). Over an order of magnitude faster when only phasing is needed. Under 20 min per sample on average across 109 deeply sequenced nanopore metagenomes. With short reads, it tracked a multi-strain *Anaerostipes hadrus* community over 636 days. — [Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:39327484%20OR%20DOI:10.1093/bioinformatics/btae252%20OR%20DOI:10.1038/s41592-022-01478-3&resultType=core&format=json)
- Available on Bioconda (`floria`); code at bluenote-1577/floria. — [Bioconda](https://anaconda.org/bioconda/floria)

**hifiasm-meta**
- Citation: Feng X, Cheng H, Portik D, Li H. *Nature Methods* 19(6):671-674 (2022), Brief Communication. DOI 10.1038/s41592-022-01478-3. HiFi only. — [Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:39327484%20OR%20DOI:10.1093/bioinformatics/btae252%20OR%20DOI:10.1038/s41592-022-01478-3&resultType=core&format=json)
- Strain handling: an optional read-selection step lowers the coverage of very abundant strains without losing reads from rare ones. A contained read is dropped only if no other similar haplotype is nearby. Unitig joining is coverage-aware, assuming unitigs from one strain have similar coverage. Unitigs from different haplotypes may be joined to patch gaps. — [arXiv 2110.08457](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.08457)
- ZymoBIOMICS D6331 (5 E. coli strains at 8% each): hifiasm-meta assembled B766 as one circular contig, B3008 in 2 contigs, and the rest fragmented. HiCanu got both B766 and B3008 circular. metaFlye got none of the 5 circular. — [arXiv 2110.08457](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.08457); [Nature Methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-022-01478-3)
- sheepA (HiFi): 125 circular near-complete contigs, against 64 for HiCanu and 31 for metaFlye. Mash divergence between all hifiasm circular contig pairs was above 1%, so no circular contigs were near-identical duplicates. — [arXiv 2110.08457](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.08457)
- Version difference: the arXiv text says 72% of metaFlye circular contigs were also circular in hifiasm-meta; the search summary of the published version says 94%. Check the journal version before quoting. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.08457) vs [Nature Methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-022-01478-3)

**metaMDBG / nanoMDBG**
- metaMDBG: Benoit G et al. *Nature Biotechnology* 42:1378-1383 (2024). HiFi. Uses a de Bruijn graph in minimizer space with multiple k sizes and a "local progressive abundance filter" that removes complex errors, inter-genomic repeats and strain variability. Strain duplicates are purged after racon-style polishing. So the design collapses or discards minor strains rather than resolving them. — [search summary of PMC11392814](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11392814); [UEA record](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/97134)
- On the E. coli-containing mock (Zymo D6331), no assembler resolved all E. coli strain diversity. metaMDBG and hifiasm-meta each circularised one strain. — [search summary of PMC11392814](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11392814)
- nanoMDBG, the extension of metaMDBG to ONT reads, is reported in *Nature Communications* (2026). R10-specific details were not verified. — [search summary; NORA reference PDF](https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/541255/1/s41467-026-69760-y_reference.pdf)
- An independent 2026 *Nature Biotechnology* evaluation (Trigodet et al., DOI 10.1038/s41587-025-02971-8, per the search summary) reported that metaMDBG produced more repeats than other assemblers, including duplicated sequence with no read support. — [Trigodet 2026 PDF](https://epic.awi.de/id/eprint/60560/1/Trigodet_2026_nature_biotechnology.pdf). Read only via search summary; treat as unverified detail.

**myloasm**
- Citation: Shaw J, Marin M, Li H. "High-resolution metagenome assembly for modern long reads with myloasm." bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.09.05.674543 (Sept 2025). The GitHub README now lists it as published in *Nature Biotechnology* (2026). The journal DOI was not verified. — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.05.674543.full.pdf); [GitHub](https://github.com/bluenote-1577/myloasm)
- Reads: ONT R10 simplex at about 97% accuracy or better (sup/hac basecalling), and PacBio HiFi. R9 is not listed. — [GitHub](https://github.com/bluenote-1577/myloasm)
- Method: a string graph simplified with polymorphic k-mers ("SNPmers") and differential abundance. — [bioRxiv search summary](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.05.674543.full.pdf)
- Strain claims: the docs say other nanopore assemblers "collapse similar genomes at > 95% ANI" while "myloasm can resolve up to 99% ANI quite confidently." The preprint reports 6 complete single-contig *Prevotella copri* genomes from one gut metagenome, 8 complete TM7 contigs at >93% similarity from an oral metagenome, and two 98%-similar *ermF* genes resolved on distinct mobile elements. — [myloasm docs](https://myloasm-docs.github.io/); [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.05.674543.full.pdf)
- Yield: about 3× more complete circular contigs than the next-best assembler on real ONT data (preprint), and "> 2-10 times more circular contigs than metaFlye" (README). — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.05.674543.full.pdf); [GitHub](https://github.com/bluenote-1577/myloasm)
- Compute: "about the same RAM as metaFlye with faster runtime", and more RAM than metaMDBG. The docs warn it may "occasionally produce chimeric misassembled contigs" and recommend the mylotools QC guide. — [myloasm docs](https://myloasm-docs.github.io/)

**Other tools seen in passing**
- devider: Shaw et al., "Long-read reconstruction of many diverse haplotypes with devider", *Genome Research* 2025. Aimed at many (more than 5) similar haplotypes, e.g. a gene or locus. It notes that earlier prokaryotic phasers had not been tested with more than 5 similar haplotypes. — [Genome Research PDF](https://genome.cshlp.org/content/genome/early/2025/09/23/gr.280510.125.full.pdf)
- HyLight: a hybrid (short + long read), strain-aware assembler for low-coverage metagenomes. — [PMC11458758](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11458758/)
- "Enhancing long-read-based strain-aware metagenome assembly" (*Frontiers in Genetics*, 2022). — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/genetics/articles/10.3389/fgene.2022.868280/epub)

### Inferences
- Fit with the danaSeq pipeline: Strainy (metaFlye GFA input) and Floria (BAM + VCF on contigs, with or without reads) are the only verified tools here that take an existing assembly or bin plus reads and return phased haplotigs or read groups. Strainberry also takes an assembly but is older: it uses wtdbg2 and diploid phasing. Strainy wants an unpolished `--keep-haplotypes` metaFlye graph, which may mean a separate metaFlye run with different flags from the production assembly.
- Rough divergence floor: phasing-based tools need enough SNV density. Strainberry starts working at about 0.4-0.5% divergence (about 99.5-99.6% ANI) and is reliable from about 0.7%. Myloasm claims to resolve strains up to 99% ANI. None of the verified sources show strain separation above 99.9% ANI from long reads alone.
- Depth: about 20× per strain appears repeatedly as the working minimum (Strainberry's empirical threshold; Strainy's default minimum unitig coverage). Strains below about 10× will mostly collapse or fragment.
- metaMDBG removes strain variation by design and metaFlye (without `--keep-haplotypes`) collapses it. Neither should be expected to give strain-resolved MAGs. Post-binning phasing, or a myloasm re-assembly of R10 reads, is the strain step.

### Gaps
- Strainy and myloasm quantitative benchmarks (mock-community completeness, error rates, divergence curves, tests against Strainberry, iGDA and hifiasm-meta) could not be read: the bioRxiv and Nature full texts returned 429 errors or redirects. Only abstract-level claims are recorded here.
- metaFlye: the citation (Kolmogorov et al., *Nature Methods* 2020) and the exact behaviour of `--keep-haplotypes` (it keeps alternative paths instead of collapsing bubbles; it does not phase) were not verified with a source this session. Only the Strainy README's recommended command is cited. There is no separate "metaFlye strain mode" in any source found.
- Not researched or verified, so no claims are made: HaploMeta; VStrains (believed to be viral, short-read); StrainXpress (believed to be short-read); Strainline (believed to be viral long-read haplotype reconstruction, Genome Biology 2022, unverified); iGDA; HaploDMF; any R10.4.1 duplex-specific strain phasing work.
- Hybrid phasing with Illumina: Floria accepts short reads and HyLight is hybrid. No benchmark of ONT + Illumina strain phasing was read.
- Multi-sample strain phasing for long reads: no verified source found. Floria was applied per sample across a time series.
- How ONT R9 vs R10 error rates limit SNV phasing: only Strainberry's qualitative statement (ONT gives worse identity and completeness because of indels) is cited. No quantitative comparison of R10.4.1 phasing accuracy was found.
